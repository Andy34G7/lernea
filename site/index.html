    Lernea

    Benchmark results for 13 subword tokenisation algorithms, trained on the same text and compared on the same held-out text.

    The algorithms are byte-level BPE with ten different merge scores, a two-stage SuperBPE, a morpheme-guided BPE and a Unigram language model. All are implemented in one Rust library with Python bindings. This page reports what was measured, in order of cost: from counting tokens to training GPT-2 small.

    Which tokenisers are compared?

    Every tokeniser starts from the 256 byte values, so any UTF-8 text encodes and decodes exactly and there is no unknown token. Before merges are learned, text is cut into pre-tokens, and no token crosses a pre-token boundary. The default pre-tokeniser isolates every whitespace run: “the cat” becomes “the”, “ ”, “cat”.

    The bottom-up variants share one training loop: score every adjacent pair \((u, v)\), merge the highest-scoring pair into a new token everywhere, repeat until the vocabulary has \(V\) tokens. They differ only in the score. Open a row for its formula and defaults.

    Also implemented and not benchmarked: Unigram sampling (forward-filtering backward-sampling over the Unigram lattice) and byte-entropy patching (a new patch starts where a byte-level Markov model's predictive entropy exceeds a threshold). BPE-dropout appears only in the proxy LM.

    How to read the numbers

    An arrow after a metric gives its direction: ↑ higher is better, ↓ lower is better. Bold marks the best value in a column.

    Bytes per token ↑
    UTF-8 bytes of held-out text divided by the number of tokens. More bytes per token means shorter sequences: more text per context window and per training step.
    Fertility ↓
    Tokens per word. Words are whitespace-separated, except Chinese and Japanese, which are counted per character.
    Word split % ↓ · Continued % ↓
    Share of words that become two or more tokens, and share of tokens that continue a word instead of starting one.
    Rényi efficiency ↑
    \(E_\alpha = H_\alpha(p) / \log_2 |V|\), where \(p\) is the token distribution on held-out text. 1 means every token is used equally often; a larger \(\alpha\) penalises a few very frequent tokens more. Zouhar et al. (2023) proposed \(\alpha = 2.5\) as a predictor of downstream quality.
    Gini ↓ · Active fraction ↑
    Inequality of token frequencies on held-out text (0 = all equal), and the share of the vocabulary that occurs there at all.
    Morph F1 ↑
    For each SIGMORPHON 2022 gold word, the offsets where the tokeniser cuts are compared with the gold morpheme boundaries. F1 per word, averaged per language, then over the 5 languages.
    Identifier F1 ↑
    The same on identifiers mined from held-out code. Gold cuts are at _ and - runs, camelCase humps and acronym ends.
    Bits per byte (BPB) ↓
    A language model's total negative log-likelihood of the validation tokens, in bits, divided by the UTF-8 bytes of that text. Per-token perplexity cannot be compared across tokenisers, because a tokeniser with longer tokens makes fewer, harder predictions. BPB charges every tokeniser for the same bytes.
    GPT-2-equivalent steps ↑
    The first step at which GPT-2's own smoothed train BPB reaches a tokeniser's final validation BPB: how many of GPT-2's steps buy the same loss.
    Tokenisation premium ↓
    Tokens spent on a language's sentences divided by tokens spent on the same sentences in English. 1.0 is parity; 2.0 means the same content costs twice the tokens.

        How much more do other languages cost than English?

        FLORES+ is professionally translated parallel text, so every language carries the same content. The premium is the tokens a tokeniser spends on a language divided by the tokens it spends on the same sentences in English. In-mix languages are part of the tokenisers' training mix; out-of-mix languages are in no tokeniser's training text.

          Known limitations

          • One scale, one split. The compression, intrinsic, throughput and proxy-LM results use one 52.5 MB train split (seed 0). The GPT-2 swap trains on different text. Tokeniser training is deterministic; only the language models have seeds.
          • PMI-ranked variants barely compress. wordpiece_pmi, wp_bpe_ft and wp_bpe_base encode at about 1.01 to 1.03 bytes per token and use a few hundred to about a thousand of their 16,384 tokens. This is the behaviour their scores define.
          • No duplicate variants. A fingerprint check found no byte-identical tokenisers among the 13 variants at any size.
          • Not benchmarked. Unigram sampling and byte-entropy patching are implemented and absent from the suite. BPE-dropout appears only in the proxy LM.
          • Proxy LM. The model has no positional encoding. With 3 seeds, the top five of the small LM are within noise; the big LM separates four variants from bpe_frequency and leaves their mutual order open.
          • Morphology. Morph F1 uses SIGMORPHON 2022 gold for 5 languages. morpheme_bpe has supervision the others do not.
          • Throughput. Single-threaded and load-dependent. Three measurements exist; compare within one setting only.
          • GPT-2 swap. 600 steps is early training, with one run per tokeniser and no seed spread.
